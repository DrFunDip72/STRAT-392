## Summary

The BYU Pathway Program partners with students to create educational and job opportunities in underserved regions, particularly Africa, through internships and business development. The program focuses on scaling existing businesses in South Africa, Kenya, and Botswana, with students applying classroom concepts to real-world challenges while receiving mentorship and potential investment from business leaders. The initiative aims to address resource constraints and economic disparities by fostering local entrepreneurship and value capture, with a pilot program starting in June and offering scholarships for financial need.

## Key Points

- BYU Pathway Program targets students to create educational and job opportunities for those without access, with 37,000 BYU students and 100,000 global participants.
- The program's largest growth area is Africa, where students operate 24 existing businesses in South Africa, focusing on scaling through consulting and business development.
- Nairobi, Kenya, is identified as the 'Silicon Savannah' for tech and startups, providing context for business operations in resource-constrained environments.
- Botswana's economic transformation from poverty to wealth through diamond value capture—processing diamonds locally instead of exporting raw materials—serves as a case study for value creation.
- Students will intern in Johannesburg, working with local businesses to improve sales, strategy, and financial operations using academic skills.
- The program includes a 'shark tank'-style investment event with business leaders to fund student-led companies, with costs subsidized by these leaders.
- Scholarships are available for merit and financial need through the Whitmore Global Business Center to support participation.
- The program is a pilot initiative, with plans to expand annually, emphasizing real-world application of classroom concepts and community impact.
- Students must complete a class exam by mid-June, with automatic grading for multiple-choice questions and partial credit for short answers.
- The instructor encourages students to seek mentors, noting that having multiple mentors can significantly impact personal and professional growth.
- Big takeaway — The BYU Pathway Program partners with students to create educational and job opportunities in underserved regions, particularly Africa, through internships and business development.
