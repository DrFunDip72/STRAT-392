## Summary

The lecture emphasizes that human value is rooted in divine potential rather than performance metrics, shifting focus from business-level strategy to corporate-level strategy. Corporate strategy involves firm scope (businesses to enter), firm boundaries (make/buy/ally decisions), and evaluating diversification value through the 'better-off test'—ensuring combined value exceeds individual units. Disney's strategic evolution, including avoiding gaming initially due to cultural incompatibility but later recommitting, illustrates the need for ongoing reassessment of diversification decisions.

## Key Points

- Concept — Firm scope: The range of businesses a corporation should operate, including vertical integration (e.g., Coke), horizontal diversification (e.g., Disney's theme parks and streaming), and geographic diversification (e.g., Harley Davidson's struggles in China).
- Concept — Firm boundaries: Operational decisions on whether to make, buy, or ally with external partners to manage resources and capabilities.
- Concept — Corporate strategy: Creating value for the whole corporation by managing multiple business units, distinct from business-level strategy focused on single-unit competitive advantage.
- Concept — Better-off test: Diversification increases value only if combined units are worth more together than separately, though synergies are hard to predict and integration costs are underestimated.
- Concept — Disney's gaming strategy: Initially avoided gaming due to cultural incompatibility and resource diversion but later recommitted as a core business, highlighting strategic reassessment over time.
- Concept — Boston Consulting Group's 2x2 matrix: Categorizes business units into stars (high market share, high growth), cash cows (low market share, low growth), question marks (high growth, low market share), and dogs (low market share, low growth).
- Concept — In-house vs. partnerships: Disney chose partnerships over full in-house development for character creation due to core competency considerations, preferring intimate partnerships with significant influence on brand.
- Concept — Liability of foreignness: Failing to adhere to local social norms (e.g., not greeting in France) can lead to being perceived as an object, resulting in lost opportunities like job applications.
- Concept — Global expansion benefits: Market growth, efficiency, scale, risk management, and learning from international experiences, but success requires minimizing cultural, administrative, geographic, and economic differences.
- Big takeaway — The lecture emphasizes that human value is rooted in divine potential rather than performance metrics, shifting focus from business-level strategy to corporate-level strategy.
