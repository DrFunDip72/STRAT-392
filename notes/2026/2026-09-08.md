# STRAT 392 — September 8, 2026

## Summary

The lecture covers strategic problem-solving techniques, emphasizing correct problem framing to avoid pitfalls like misdefinition, narrow framing, and cultural missteps. Key methods include the four S method (stating, structuring, solving, selling), the Tosca framework for problem definition, and issue trees with mutually exclusive branches. Real-world examples from music industry shifts, JCPenney, microfinance, and Starbucks France illustrate the importance of context, iterative learning, and avoiding assumptions.

## Key Points

- Concept — Strategic problem solving avoids over-analysis or hasty decisions by correctly framing problems, as seen in Apple's digital music shift versus record labels' resistance.
- Concept — Four S method: Stating the problem, structuring it via five steps (trouble, owner, success criteria, constraints, actors), solving, and selling the solution.
- Concept — Tosca framework: Defines problems through symptoms (trouble), perspective (owner), desired outcomes (success criteria), limitations (constraints), and stakeholders (actors).
- Concept — Issue tree method: Breaks problems into mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive sub-issues to ensure comprehensive exploration without overlap.
- Concept — BEES principle: Avoid approaches that violate this principle (e.g., focusing on revenue without addressing root causes).
- Concept — Hypothesis pyramid: Structures complex problems into smaller, manageable hypotheses for iterative analysis.
- Concept — Optimal cold contact message length: 20-25 words to ensure clarity and avoid overwhelming recipients, with response rates dropping significantly above 100-150 words.
- Concept — Pyramid principle: Start with core message first to capture attention in high-traffic environments like managerial inboxes.
- Concept — Grouping structure: Core message followed by supporting reasons (e.g., 'A, B, C') for direct communication.
- Concept — Argument structure: Situation, complication, and resolution for skeptical audiences needing nuanced explanations.
- Pitfall — Narrow framing: JCPenney's CEO misapplied Apple's customer model, assuming customers were similar without adapting to cultural context.
- Pitfall — Cultural missteps: Microfinance initiatives in Bangladesh failed due to ignoring lactose intolerance and yogurt's perishable nature in remote areas.
- Pitfall — Miscommunication: U.S. scientist's 'fat makes you fat' narrative dominated over complex British research on sugar, showing framing's impact on public perception.
- Pitfall — Survivor bias: Analysis focusing only on successful cases (e.g., customers who stayed with a product) while ignoring those who left.
- Big takeaway — The lecture covers strategic problem-solving techniques, emphasizing correct problem framing to avoid pitfalls like misdefinition, narrow framing, and cultural missteps.
