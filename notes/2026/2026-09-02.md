## Key Points

- Concept — Business strategy: A plan to gain and sustain competitive advantage through choices aligned with mission and objectives
- Concept — Value creation: Depends on interactions between external environment conditions, resource endowments, and capabilities (e.g., preferences, social capital, information)
- Concept — Market dynamics: Value increases with market size and competition, but time reduces information asymmetry
- Concept — Strategic trade-offs: Choosing one action (e.g., marketing) requires sacrificing another (e.g., reinvestment) to maintain competitive advantage
- Concept — Context dependency: Strategy must align with a company's stage and values (e.g., Amazon's customer-centric ecosystem vs. luxury brands' exclusivity)
- Concept — Sustainable competitive advantage: Unique value that others cannot easily replicate or imitate, as seen in Amazon's local fulfillment and luxury brands' private sales
- Concept — Strategic alignment: Decisions must support overall company goals, such as customer retention and ecosystem growth
- Concept — Team composition: Five-person teams with at least two to three majors to ensure diverse perspectives
- Concept — AI usage: Must be a tool, not a substitute, in academic work
- Concept — Class activity: Students submit questions for discussion, with top questions forming the class discussion topic
- Concept — Short-term vs. long-term: Initial strategy gains may not sustain competitive advantage without replication barriers
- Concept — Reinvestment strategy: Amazon reinvests earnings to grow long-term while maintaining customer-centric value
- Big takeaway — The business strategy course introduces students to strategic thinking through a candy trading game, emphasizing value creation via external conditions, resources, and capabilities.
